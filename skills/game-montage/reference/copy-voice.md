# On-screen text and upload copy

Generic guidance. **The channel's own voice (language, slang, reaction examples, phrases the owner likes or bans) lives in `<profile_dir>/copy-voice.md`**; read it first when it exists, and write it as you learn the owner's taste when it does not.

## One voice per channel
- Agree once on the language(s): which language the player's own reactions and notes use, which language titles and descriptions use, and whether thumbnails follow the same rule. Then keep it the same across videos.
- Game terms stay as they are in the game: game, boss, item, place and chapter names, in-game quotes. Music credit lines stay verbatim.
- Never add translated subtitles over game dialogue when the game shows its own subtitles.
- Write titles, hooks and descriptions the way the owner would say them out loud to a friend, ideally in the owner's own words. If a draft reads like ad copy, rewrite it plainer.

## On-screen text
- Most moments need no text. A quiet beat, clean gameplay or no edit is often the strongest choice, and unedited stretches give the punchy ones contrast.
- Text adds a perspective, timing or punchline; it never narrates what the viewer can already see.
- Keep it small and out of the way of the gameplay and the game's HUD. Counters can be an icon + number (`hud` with `"minimal": true`).
- Reaction captions escalate only as far as the footage supports (a bigger reaction on every failure wears out fast), and should not make the player look incompetent in every clip.
- Lyric words on screen come only from words the owner typed (see SKILL.md, ground rules).
- Per-game text rules (none / song words / SFX words / reactions) belong in `<profile_dir>/games.md`.

## Truth rules
- Separate **observed fact** (seen in the footage or HUD, or said by the owner), **interpretation** (a creative reading, e.g. frustration) and **suggestion** (an overlay or effect). Present interpretations as suggestions until the owner OKs them.
- The owner is the player: reactions they approve are theirs. Never present an unapproved reaction as something the player actually said or felt.
- Never invent boss names, deaths, wins, drops, dialogue, difficulty or outcomes. Unknown = leave it out, mark it unverified, or ask.
- If footage is missing (the recording was cut), say so in a short player note instead of faking the moment.

## Upload metadata
- Title: the hook plus the game name as written in the game (and the boss when it is the subject), early in the title.
- Description: 1-3 plain sentences on what happens. No lists of three, no rhetorical build-ups, at most one or two casual questions.
- Hashtags and tags: [hashtags.md](hashtags.md).
- Offer a few title options and let the owner pick.

## Avoid (titles, captions, descriptions)
Unless the footage truly earns it: "you won't believe what happens next", "most epic fight ever", "hardest boss in the world", slogan-style parallel lines, dramatic headline phrasing, forced hype, engagement bait, keyword stuffing, excessive hashtags, generic like/comment/subscribe filler, and meme sounds / zooms / shakes on everything just because the tool can.
